# Research Questions

Updated: 10 September 2026.

Field 01 remains a research program. The immediate objective is to connect clearly specified mathematical models to physical questions without treating interpretation as a derived result.

## Available Starting Points

- The [particle paper](articles/particle_as_closed_wave_en.pdf) states localization, regularity, finite-energy, and stability requirements.
- The [formalization paper](articles/field01_formalization_program_en.pdf) distinguishes standard scalar/gauge models from the proposed open and closed interpretations.
- The [August numerical report](articles/fixed_background_optimization_v0_4_en.pdf) documents a restricted fixed-background profile calculation. Its checkpoint and acceptance criteria are unchanged.

## Next Mathematical Questions

1. Specify the field content, action, normalization, boundary conditions, and physical observables for each candidate model.
2. Identify a physical massless open mode rather than equating it with a scalar phase at zero modulus.
3. Determine whether a non-removable interaction can connect that mode to a localized sector.
4. Establish whether the resulting configuration is localized in three spatial dimensions, has finite energy, and is stable in the relevant perturbation classes.
5. Derive any proposed charge, spin, or mass from the specified dynamics rather than from winding or geometric imagery alone.

Each calculation should state its question, assumptions, supplied data, and acceptance criteria before its outcome is interpreted. A new sector is not validated by the existence of a benchmark in another sector.

## Separate Open Questions

Memory requires a precise state space and an equivalence or retention rule. Boundary recording requires a defined map and observables. The horizon proposal additionally requires comparison with semiclassical radiation and black-hole thermodynamics; no horizon law is inferred from the profile calculations.

## Publication Practice

Public updates contain readable papers, necessary data, reproducibility instructions, and concise statements of scope. Numerical release numbers and article editions are tracked separately. Earlier translations are labelled rather than presented as synchronized editions.
