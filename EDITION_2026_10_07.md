# Methodological Paper Edition — 7 October 2026

This staging edition adds **Reading a Past Label versus Updating a Surface Record: An Explicit Bulk--Boundary Example**, version 0.1.

## Positioning

The paper is motivated by a question in the broader Field 01 programme: when can an accessible boundary observable identify a selected past label, and when is it sufficient to predict a response to a specified future input? The paper treats this as a self-contained mathematical problem in a fixed massless wave-field/boundary-oscillator model. Field 01 supplies motivation only; the paper does not derive its equations from, or test, a cosmological Field 01 proposal.

## Results and Limits

The manuscript distinguishes three statements with different preparation families and access assumptions: finite-time reading of an equal-energy pulse-order label; failure of a scalar record to close under a common input on a broader reachable family; and controlled closure of a two-component relative record when the phase reference and next input are supplied. It does not claim minimality, autonomous input generation, or persistent physical storage.

This edition is an analytic methodological example, not a new numerical release, a demonstrated physical memory mechanism, or a claim of established publication novelty. The cited comparisons locate the example within existing work; they do not establish novelty.

## Related-Work Check

The comparison was checked against the cited primary papers on a boundary oscillator coupled to a field, scalar fields coupled to endpoint point masses, dynamical Wentzell boundary conditions, exact lumpability, and predictive input--output summaries. These sources establish substantial prior work on the model classes and general ideas used here. The manuscript therefore presents its pulse-order identity, reachable scalar-nonclosure witnesses, and controlled relative-record update as a worked example under explicit assumptions, not as a priority claim.

This was a bounded check of identified sources, not a systematic literature review. It cannot establish that no earlier work contains the same or a closely related construction.
